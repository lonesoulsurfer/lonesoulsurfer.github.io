This is my 3rd installment on my lighter made from a shotgun shell!

All of the improvements were suggested by viewers on my YouTube page.

The first being that the lighter needs to be able to stand up by itself. In previous versions there was a screw in the bottom which is where you filled the lighter up with fluid and the lighter couldn't stand up. That has been removed in the 3rd version.

The second suggestion was a way to connect the cap section to the lighter. Previously, you had to un-screw the cap and hold it in your hand whilst you lit the lighter. So instead of a screw cap, I built a lever section that allows you to take the cap off the wick without having to un-screw it, and it stays attached to the lighter.

It does add some complexity to the overall build but the functionality and overall aesthetics are definitely improved.

The first thing you need to do is to modify a shotgun shell cap to enable the schrader valve and sparkwheel to be able to be attached to it. We'll start first with removing the primer and enlarging the hole

STEPS:

- Place a shotgun shell on top of the vice with the vice slightly open.
- Use a hole punch (or small phillips head or whatever else will work) and place it inside the shell on top of the primer.
- Use a hammer and lightly tap the punch to remove the primer
- Place the shell into the vice so the cap section is close to the actual vice
- Use a drill piece to drill out the hole so it is large enough to fit the Schrader valve into the top of it


## Step 2: Modifying a Shotgun Shell Cap - Part 2

![Step 2: Modifying a Shotgun Shell Cap - Part 2 image 1](images/step03_01.jpg)

![Step 2: Modifying a Shotgun Shell Cap - Part 2 image 2](images/step03_02.jpg)

![Step 2: Modifying a Shotgun Shell Cap - Part 2 image 3](images/step03_03.jpg)

![Step 2: Modifying a Shotgun Shell Cap - Part 2 image 4](images/step03_04.jpg)

The next thing you need to do to the cap is to file a small groove into the top lip of the cap. This will allow the sparkwheel will sit against the cap nicely.

STEPS:

- Use a small, round file and start to file a section of the cap lip away
- Keep on going until the sparkwheel body sits flat against the cap.
- You will also need to file away a small section of the square part of the sparkwheel.  This is so it sits flat against the shell.  Use a small, flat file to do this.  This section will also be used later to be able to 'lock' the Schrader cap into place on the wick so it should just sit on the shotgun shell as in the last image


## Step 3: Removing the Modified Cap From the Shell

![Step 3: Removing the Modified Cap From the Shell image 1](images/step04_01.jpg)

![Step 3: Removing the Modified Cap From the Shell image 2](images/step04_02.jpg)

![Step 3: Removing the Modified Cap From the Shell image 3](images/step04_03.jpg)

![Step 3: Removing the Modified Cap From the Shell image 4](images/step04_04.jpg)

Now that all of the mods have been performed to the cap, it's time to remove it from the shell.

STEPS:

- Place the shell into a vice so the cap is facing out
- Heat the top of the cap with a small blow torch (you could probably do this will a lighter as well!).  Heat for about 10 seconds.
- Use a pair of plyers to carefully remove the cap. make sure you don't grip the plyers too hard or you'll squash the cap!

![Step 4: Soldering the Sparkwheel to the Modified Cap image 9](images/step05_09.jpg)

Time to do the first bit of soldering.

STEPS:

- First thing to do is to remove all of the parts that make up the sparkwheel so you are only left with the body section.  Take note of the orientation of the actual sparkwheel as it needs to be put back in the same way or it won't spark
- Add some flux to the side of the cap where the sparkwheel is to be soldered.
- Place the sparkwheel against the side of the cap and carefully secure it in a vice. Make sure that the sparkwheel is straight against the cap and everything is lined up nicely. if it isn't you'll end up with a crooked sparkwheel.
- Add a little piece of solder to the section to be soldered and heat with a blow torch. remember to not apply heat directly at first to the solder and heat up the cap and sparkwheel first
- Leave to cool and then check to make sure it all worked as planned. If you do find that it is crooked or not right, you can always just heat it up and try again
- Oh and put the sparkwheel back together!

The valve itself comes with everything you need to be able to add it to the cap. However, you need to do a couple things to it first before adding it.

STEPS:

- Remove the nuts and seals from the Schrader valve. Keep them though as you will need to use some of the parts later
- Remove the valve section inside with some pliers or scissors
- As the valve is too long, you will to trim off some of the threaded section. place it in a vice (careful as the metal on the valves is quite soft.  I used some scrap wood to ensure it wasn't damaged) and remove about 18mm off. I used a small cutting wheel on my dremel to do this but you could also use a small metal saw.
- Next, in order to be able to fit the rope (wick) inside the value, you need to enlarge the hole slightly. Place the valve into a vice and with a drill bit slightly larger then the inside diameter, drill through the valve.
- Lastly, file any burrs on the end of the valve and add the cap to ensure it fits right.

## Step 7: Adding a Shotgun Shell 'sleeve' to the Copper Pipe

![Step 7: Adding a Shotgun Shell 'sleeve' to the Copper Pipe image 1](images/step08_01.jpg)

![Step 7: Adding a Shotgun Shell 'sleeve' to the Copper Pipe image 2](images/step08_02.jpg)

![Step 7: Adding a Shotgun Shell 'sleeve' to the Copper Pipe image 4](images/step08_04.jpg)

![Step 7: Adding a Shotgun Shell 'sleeve' to the Copper Pipe image 5](images/step08_05.jpg)

![Step 7: Adding a Shotgun Shell 'sleeve' to the Copper Pipe image 6](images/step08_06.jpg)

![Step 7: Adding a Shotgun Shell 'sleeve' to the Copper Pipe image 7](images/step08_07.jpg)

![Step 7: Adding a Shotgun Shell 'sleeve' to the Copper Pipe image 8](images/step08_08.jpg)

![Step 7: Adding a Shotgun Shell 'sleeve' to the Copper Pipe image 9](images/step08_09.jpg)

The copper pipe adds strength to the lighter and ensures good seals are created. Now if you wanted to you could just skip this section and make a lighter that has a metal look instead. I did this in my prototype and liked the ascetics. However, this is a 'shotgun shell' lighter so adding te shell section was a no brainer for me.

STEPS:

- You first need to remove the cap off the end of the shell.  Instead of squashing the shell in a vice which could damage it, I just held it in my hand whist I heated up the cap.  You don't want to add a lot of heat as the aim here is to have a shotgun shell sleeve that is pretty much intact and not melted.
- Use a pair of plyers to carefully take off the cap
- Place the copper pipe inside the shell sleeve.  You may need to place it into a vice and push the pipe into the sleeve
- once the pipe is inside the sleeve, you'll need to trim the ends up of the shotgun shell.  I used a exacto knife to do this.  You should also file around the cut section with a small file as well.  This will help with adding the caps to the ends of the sleeve
- You can now put the top section of the lighter onto the body.  To do this place the cap onto the sleeve/pipe section and push it into place.  It's a tight fit so to make sure it is secure you can use a vice to do this.  Don't though just stick it into a vice and start squeezing it as you'll bend the cap from the middle.  Instead, use a small piece of pipe that fits over the Schrader valve and place this along with the copper section into a vice and slowly close the vice up.
- Once the cap is in place, you van then tighten-up the nut on the Schrader valve.
- Lastly, keep the cap that you took off the shell as you can use this for the bottom section of the lighter later on.


## Step 8: Making the Slider Section for Opening/closing the Wick Cap

![Step 8: Making the Slider Section for Opening/closing the Wick Cap image 1](images/step09_01.jpg)

![Step 8: Making the Slider Section for Opening/closing the Wick Cap image 2](images/step09_02.jpg)

![Step 8: Making the Slider Section for Opening/closing the Wick Cap image 3](images/step09_03.jpg)

![Step 8: Making the Slider Section for Opening/closing the Wick Cap image 4](images/step09_04.jpg)

![Step 8: Making the Slider Section for Opening/closing the Wick Cap image 5](images/step09_05.jpg)

![Step 8: Making the Slider Section for Opening/closing the Wick Cap image 6](images/step09_06.jpg)

So the biggest difference to the other versions is the inclusion of a mechanism where you remove the cap on the wick without having to un-screw it. This is a huge benefit as you could easily lose the cap and it makes it so much quicker and easier to light.

The first thing we will start with is the copper ring that goes around the sleeve and also start to make the rod section that joins up to the top of the cap.

STEPS:

- The first thing is to measure and cut a piece of the brass tube. It should be the same size the the larger section of the barrel hinge.
- Now you can use the piece of cut tube to measure how big you need to cut the copper pipe to make the ring
- I used a pipe cutter to cut the copper.  you could use a grinder with a cutting wheel to do it as well.  I find though you get a good straight cut using a pipe cutter
- Next, remove any burrs from the inside of the ring and sand the inside and cut sections smooth.  Any burrs inside the ring will mark the shotgun shell sleeve.
- Cut the ring down the middle and again remove any burrs.  You will need to spread open the ring in order for it to fit over the sleeve.  The problem is once you open it up it won't be round.  I have a small metal bender which I used to make it the right size but you could use a rubber mallet and another piece of pipe to get it to the shape you need.  It needs to fit over the sleeve and be able to move up and down it smoothly.


## Step 9: Soldering the Brass Tube to the Ring

![Step 9: Soldering the Brass Tube to the Ring image 1](images/step10_01.jpg)

You now need to solder the copper tube to the ring section. It's pretty straight forward but you do need to make sure that you solder the tube as straight as possible.

STEPS:

- Place the ring and tube into a vice.  Make sure that you have the tube as straight as possible.  It's hard to see when it is in the vice so you can add a couple lines with a marker to the ring to help you align the tube
- Add some flux and solder and use a small blow torch to heat up the ring and tube.
- Once cool, place it onto the shotgun shell sleeve and make sure it moves well still.
- Lastly, give it a polish.


## Step 10: Making the Rod Arm Section

![Step 10: Making the Rod Arm Section image 1](images/step11_01.jpg)

![Step 10: Making the Rod Arm Section image 2](images/step11_02.jpg)

![Step 10: Making the Rod Arm Section image 3](images/step11_03.jpg)

![Step 10: Making the Rod Arm Section image 4](images/step11_04.jpg)

![Step 10: Making the Rod Arm Section image 5](images/step11_05.jpg)

![Step 10: Making the Rod Arm Section image 6](images/step11_06.jpg)

![Step 10: Making the Rod Arm Section image 7](images/step11_07.jpg)

The rod section is made using a barrel hinge, more brass tubing and some brass rod. It is a little finicky as you need to use superglue and get everything aligned right. However, if you do find that you glued something in wrong, you can always just heat it up and the glue will melt.

STEPS:

- Place the barrel hinge into the brass tube on the ring.  making sure that the hinge section is sticking out the top.  Ben the top hinge section down and then add some superglue to the bottom tube section to glue it into place
- Cut another piece of tube ...mm long.  Remove any burrs, add a few drops of superglue inside the tube and push it onto the top of the barrel hinge.  Again make sure that the hinge is bent as you don't want to push the tube too far on the hinge or it won't open.
- Ok - so now grab the brass tube, place the end into a vice and bend it 90 degrees.  You will be able to do this with your hand.
- Cut the bent section off, remove any burrs and place it into the top of the brass tube.
- Place the ring onto the shotgun shell sleeve and make sure that the Schrader valve cap is on the top.  Now you need to work out how much of the bent rod section you need to cut in order for it to sit flush against the cap.  This is an important step so take you time and make sure that the rod just touches the cap.
- When the bend rod is sitting right, you can then superglue the bend rod into the brass tube


## Step 11: Soldering the Rod Arm Section to the Schrader Cap

![Step 11: Soldering the Rod Arm Section to the Schrader Cap image 1](images/step12_01.jpg)

![Step 11: Soldering the Rod Arm Section to the Schrader Cap image 2](images/step12_02.jpg)

![Step 11: Soldering the Rod Arm Section to the Schrader Cap image 3](images/step12_03.jpg)

This is the last of the soldering but it can be a little tricky. As mentioned, superglue doesn't like to be heated up so there is a chance that the bend rod could become loose. If does happen then I would suggest pulling the bent rod and soldering to the Schrader cap separately.

STEPS:

- Place the lighter in a vice with the bent rod and Schrader valve facing up.
- Add a little flux to the parts that are to be soldered together
- Use a mini blow torch to heat the bent rod and cap up.
- Add some solder to the parts and solder them together.


## Step 12: Creating the Wick

![Step 12: Creating the Wick image 1](images/step13_01.jpg)

![Step 12: Creating the Wick image 2](images/step13_02.jpg)

![Step 12: Creating the Wick image 3](images/step13_03.jpg)

![Step 12: Creating the Wick image 4](images/step13_04.jpg)

![Step 12: Creating the Wick image 5](images/step13_05.jpg)

![Step 12: Creating the Wick image 6](images/step13_06.jpg)

The wick is made from 100% cotton rope called sash rope. I used 5mm rope which is the thinnest I could get. It means that it is a tight fit within the Schrader valve but it will fit. Adding some thin wire to the rope helps to wind it into the valve

STEPS:

- Cut a length of rope about 200mm long.
- Next cut a length of wire about 300mm long
- Start to wind the wire around the rope tightly about half way up the rope.
- Once you get near the end of the rope, wrap the wire tightly a couple of times around the rope and then trim the wire.  You can then trim the rope threads as well which will help be able to thread it no the valve
- Now push the end of the rope (the end with the wire around it) into the top of the valve and then start to twist it.  The wire around the rope acts a bit like a screw and you'll be able to screw the wire into the valve.
- Use a pair of pliers to pull the rope through from the bottom.  The wired rope section should stay inside the shotgun shell and the non-wired section of the rope should sit out the top
- Trim the wire at the bottom of the rope and fray the rope a little at the end.


## Step 13: Adding Cotton Wadding & Lighter Fluid

![Step 13: Adding Cotton Wadding & Lighter Fluid image 1](images/step14_01.jpg)

![Step 13: Adding Cotton Wadding & Lighter Fluid image 2](images/step14_02.jpg)

![Step 13: Adding Cotton Wadding & Lighter Fluid image 3](images/step14_03.jpg)

![Step 13: Adding Cotton Wadding & Lighter Fluid image 5](images/step14_05.jpg)

![Step 13: Adding Cotton Wadding & Lighter Fluid image 6](images/step14_06.jpg)

![Step 13: Adding Cotton Wadding & Lighter Fluid image 7](images/step14_07.jpg)

![Step 13: Adding Cotton Wadding & Lighter Fluid image 8](images/step14_08.jpg)

![Step 13: Adding Cotton Wadding & Lighter Fluid image 9](images/step14_09.jpg)

![Step 13: Adding Cotton Wadding & Lighter Fluid image 10](images/step14_10.jpg)

STEPS:

- Grab some cotton balls and tear a small part off
- Place it into the copper tube in the shotgun shell and push it into the tube using a thin screwdriver or something similar.
- Keep on adding small pieces of cotton inside the tube, making sure that the rope is kept centred within the tube.
- Pack the cotton tightly inside the tube and keep adding until it reaches the top of the tube.
- Fill the bottom shotgun shell cap with a little bit of cotton as well.
- Start to fill the inside of the tube with zippo fluid. Make sue that the top is off the lighter as it will help pull through the fluid through the lighter.
- Once the wick starts to look wet, push on the bottom cap and put the lid back onto the wick.
- Now the big moment!! Time to strike the sparkwheel and light the lighter for the first time!  Lift up the ring so the cap wick is removed and hit the spark wheel.
